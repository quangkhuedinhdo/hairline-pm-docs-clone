Male pattern baldness — clinically known as androgenetic alopecia — is the most prevalent form of hair loss worldwide, affecting approximately 85% of men by the age of 50. Unlike sudden or patchy hair loss, it follows a predictable progression: a receding hairline, thinning at the crown, and eventual loss across the top of the scalp. The good news is that it is one of the most treatable forms of hair loss, particularly when addressed early.

Section 3

What causes male pattern baldness?

Male pattern baldness is not a single cause but a convergence of genetic sensitivity, hormonal action, and the natural aging of hair follicles.

1

Genetics (Androgenetic Alopecia)

The primary driver. Inherited sensitivity in hair follicles to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) causes them to shrink over time — a process called follicular miniaturisation. The gene is polygenic, meaning it can come from either parent.

📄 NCBI — Genetics of Androgenetic Alopecia (2022)
2

DHT (Dihydrotestosterone)

Testosterone converts to DHT via the enzyme 5-alpha reductase. In men with the genetic predisposition, DHT binds to androgen receptors in follicles at the hairline and crown, progressively shortening the hair growth cycle.

📄 Journal of Dermatology — DHT and Hair Follicle Receptor Sensitivity (2021)
3

Age

Hair follicle sensitivity to DHT increases with age. Most men see first signs of recession between their mid-20s and mid-30s. The rate of progression varies widely — some men reach Norwood Stage 7 by age 40; others remain at Stage 2–3 indefinitely.

📄 International Journal of Dermatology (2020)
4

Contributing factors

While genetics and DHT are primary, lifestyle factors can accelerate loss: chronic stress, inadequate nutrition (especially iron and biotin deficiency), scalp inflammation, and certain medications.

📄 Dermatology Reports — Modifiable Risk Factors (2023)

Section 4

How is it diagnosed?

Male pattern baldness is usually diagnosed clinically — no blood test required. A qualified provider will typically follow these steps:

1

Visual examination & Norwood Scale classification

The clinician assesses hairline, crown density and recession pattern. The Hamilton–Norwood Scale (Stages I–VII) classifies the degree of loss and tracks progression over time.

2

Trichoscopy (dermoscopy)

A handheld dermatoscope magnifies the scalp surface to assess hair diameter variability, follicular unit density, and perifollicular signs — confirming miniaturisation without a biopsy.

3

Medical history review

The clinician will ask about family history, onset timeline, recent medications (finasteride, minoxidil, anabolic steroids), dietary changes, and significant stress events.

4

Blood tests (if indicated)

Not routinely required for classic MPB, but ordered if the pattern is atypical: thyroid panel, ferritin, full blood count, testosterone and SHBG levels.

5

3D head scan (via Hairline app)

Hairline's in-app 3D scan captures precise scalp topology and donor density — providing an objective baseline that tracks progress across treatments over time.

Treatment options

Several evidence-based treatments exist for male pattern baldness. The right choice depends on the stage of loss, donor density, budget and preference. Treatments often work best in combination.

✂️
Surgical
FUE Hair Transplant

Individual follicular units extracted from the donor area and implanted into thinning regions. Permanent results.

From£3,000 – £12,000
💊
Medical · Rx
Finasteride

Oral DHT-blocker. Slows progression in ~9 out of 10 men. Requires ongoing use to maintain results.

From£20 – £50 / month
💧
OTC · Popular
Minoxidil

Topical solution or foam applied to the scalp. Stimulates follicular blood flow. Works well alongside finasteride.

From£15 – £40 / month
🩸
Clinical
PRP Therapy

Platelet-rich plasma injected into the scalp to stimulate dormant follicles. Often combined with transplant procedures.

From£500 – £2,500 / course
🎨
Cosmetic
Scalp Micropigmentation

Micro-needling pigment deposits that simulate the appearance of hair follicles. Non-surgical, long-lasting cosmetic solution.

From£800 – £3,000
🔬
Surgical · New
DHI Hair Transplant

Direct Hair Implantation — a refined FUE technique using a Choi implanter pen for higher density placement.

From£4,000 – £14,000

Section 6

Prevention & lifestyle

Genetics cannot be changed — but a healthy scalp environment, early intervention, and lifestyle factors can meaningfully slow progression.

🥗

Nutrition

  • Ensure adequate iron and ferritin levels — deficiency accelerates shedding
  • Maintain protein intake ≥1.2 g/kg/day for follicular support
  • Zinc supports follicular cell function — found in seeds, legumes, meat
  • Avoid crash diets and prolonged caloric restriction
🧘

Stress management

  • Chronic cortisol elevation is linked to telogen effluvium — temporary shedding
  • Regular aerobic exercise reduces DHT conversion indirectly
  • Sleep 7–9 hours: growth hormone is released in deep sleep
  • Treat anxiety or burnout — stress-pattern loss is often reversible
🧴

Scalp health

  • Avoid high-alcohol shampoos that strip the scalp's natural oils
  • Scalp massage 4 min/day shown to increase hair thickness in studies
  • Treat dandruff or seborrheic dermatitis promptly — inflammation impairs follicles
  • Avoid tight hairstyles for extended periods (traction effect)
💉

Early medical intervention

  • Start finasteride at first signs of recession — most effective before significant loss
  • Minoxidil is OTC and safe for early preventive use
  • Combination therapy outperforms either treatment alone
  • Book a consultation before loss reaches Norwood Stage 4+

Frequently asked questions

At what age does male pattern baldness start?

▾
Male pattern baldness can begin as early as the late teens or early 20s, though the most common onset is between 25 and 35. By age 50, approximately 85% of men show significant signs. Early onset does not necessarily mean faster progression — but earlier treatment generally yields better outcomes.

Is male pattern baldness permanent?

▾
Untreated, MPB is a progressive condition — follicles that have miniaturised significantly will not regrow hair without intervention. However, hair transplantation can restore permanent hair in areas that have experienced loss, using DHT-resistant follicles from the donor zone.

Does finasteride stop hair loss completely?

▾
Finasteride stops or significantly slows progression in approximately 9 out of 10 men and leads to visible regrowth in roughly two-thirds of users over 12–24 months. Results are contingent on continued use — stopping finasteride typically reverses gains within 12 months.

Can stress cause male pattern baldness?

▾
Stress does not cause androgenetic alopecia — the underlying cause is genetic. However, severe or chronic stress can trigger telogen effluvium, a different and temporary form of widespread shedding that can appear simultaneously with MPB. Managing stress is beneficial for overall scalp health but will not reverse genetic hair loss.

How much does hair transplant surgery cost?

▾
In the UK, FUE hair transplant costs typically range from £3,000 to £12,000 depending on the number of grafts required, the clinic's location, and the surgeon's experience. Procedures performed in Turkey or Dubai by vetted surgeons often cost 50–70% less. Hairline provides personalised cost estimates based on your Norwood stage and donor density.
