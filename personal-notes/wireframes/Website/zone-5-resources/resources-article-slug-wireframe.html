Key Takeaways

What you need to know about FUE before reading on

  • ✓ FUE extracts individual follicles — no linear scar, faster recovery than FUT.
  • ✓ Full results appear between 12–18 months post-procedure.
  • ✓ Cost ranges from £3,000–£15,000+ depending on graft count, location and surgeon.
  • ✓ Not everyone is a candidate — donor density and loss stability are the key gates.

What is Follicular Unit Extraction (FUE)?

FUE — Follicular Unit Extraction — is a hair transplant technique in which individual hair follicles are removed one by one from a donor area (typically the back or sides of the scalp) and implanted into areas of thinning or baldness. 1 International Society of Hair Restoration Surgery (ISHRS). FUE: Consensus statement on technique nomenclature. 2023.

Unlike FUT (Follicular Unit Transplantation), FUE requires no strip excision of scalp tissue — meaning no linear scar, less post-operative discomfort, and a faster return to daily activity. As of 2025, FUE is the most widely performed hair transplant technique worldwide. 2 ISHRS Practice Census 2025: FUE accounts for 72% of all hair restoration procedures performed globally.

"The biggest misconception patients bring to consultation is that FUE is 'scarless'. It's scar-minimal — and for most people that distinction is academic by month six."
— Dr. Reza Ahmadi, Consultant Hair Restoration Surgeon

How does FUE work?

An FUE procedure typically unfolds in three phases, often over a single day for sessions under 2,000 grafts, or across two consecutive days for larger ones.

1. Consultation & pre-procedure planning

Before any procedure, your surgeon assesses your hair loss pattern (Norwood-Hamilton scale for men; Ludwig scale for women), evaluates donor-zone density and quality, and sets realistic expectations. On Hairline, your 3D head scan provides an objective baseline that surgeons use to build a digital plan before you meet in person. 3 Rassman WR et al. Follicular unit extraction: minimally invasive surgery for hair transplantation. Dermatologic Surgery. 2002;28(8):720–728.

ℹ️
What your Hairline 3D scan measures Scalp topography, hair density per cm², miniaturisation rate and donor reserve — all without a physical consultation. Your matched provider receives this data before your first appointment.

2. The procedure itself

On the day, the donor area is shaved and local anaesthetic applied. Using a micro-punch tool (0.6–1.0 mm diameter), the surgeon extracts individual follicular units — each containing 1–4 hairs. Grafts are stored in a hypothermic solution to preserve viability. Recipient sites are created using micro-blades angled to replicate natural growth direction, then the grafts are implanted. Grafts outside the body for more than 4–6 hours have significantly reduced survival rates.

3. Immediate aftercare

Most patients experience mild swelling, redness and tightness in donor and recipient areas for 3–5 days. Transplanted hairs typically shed between weeks 2–6 — this is normal and expected. The follicles remain intact; new growth begins around the 3-month mark.

Who is a good candidate for FUE?

FUE works best with sufficient, stable donor hair. Candidacy is not binary — a surgeon evaluates multiple factors together.

Factor Ideal for FUE May complicate FUE
Hair loss pattern Norwood II–V, stable for 2+ years Active, rapidly progressive loss
Donor density >60 follicular units / cm² Low donor density — limits graft count
Hair characteristics Coarse, curly, dark on light skin Fine, straight, light hair on light skin
Age 25+ (loss pattern established) Under 23 (pattern may still progress)
Health No active autoimmune conditions Alopecia areata, active scalp conditions

Side effects & risks

FUE is considered safe when performed by a qualified surgeon in an appropriate setting. All risks below are drawn from peer-reviewed literature and should be discussed at your consultation.

  • Temporary shock loss — native hairs near the recipient area may shed and regrow. Common, almost always temporary.
  • Folliculitis — small pimple-like inflammation around new grafts. Usually resolves without treatment.
  • Low graft survival — typically linked to poor handling or implantation outside the 4–6 hour viability window.
  • Unnatural-looking results — almost always a technique or design issue. Surgeon selection is the primary mitigation.
  • Donor area depletion — over-harvesting limits options for future sessions.

Frequently asked questions

Component · FAQ Accordion · schema-marked-up (SEO §7.1 — FAQ block)
Is FUE permanent? ▲
Transplanted follicles come from the permanent donor zone — they are genetically programmed not to miniaturise. Once established, results are considered permanent. However, you may continue to lose native (non-transplanted) hair over time, which can affect appearance and may require future sessions.
How long does the FUE procedure take? ▼
A session of 2,000 grafts typically takes 6–8 hours including extraction, site creation and implantation. Larger sessions (3,500+ grafts) are often split over two consecutive days to maintain graft viability.
Will I need to shave my head? ▼
For standard FUE, the donor area is shaved to 1–2 mm. Some clinics offer 'unshaven FUE' (U-FUE), which extracts from beneath longer native hair and avoids a visibly shaved patch — at higher cost and longer procedure time.
Can I fly after an FUE transplant? ▼
Most surgeons recommend waiting at least 5–7 days before flying. Cabin pressure changes and dry air are not believed to be harmful in themselves, but early travel increases infection risk and makes proper aftercare more difficult. Hairline Travel builds in a rest period before your return flight.
What is the difference between FUE and DHI? ▼
DHI (Direct Hair Implantation) is a variant of FUE that uses a pen-like implanter tool (Choi pen) to extract and implant follicles in a single step, without pre-made recipient sites. The key claimed benefit is reduced time outside the body per graft — though evidence on survival rate differences is mixed.
