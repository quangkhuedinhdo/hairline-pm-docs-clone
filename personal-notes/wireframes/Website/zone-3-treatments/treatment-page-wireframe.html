What is FUE hair transplant?

✓ Medically reviewed · Dr. Sarah Chen, FRCS · Last updated May 2026

Follicular Unit Extraction — commonly called FUE — is the current gold standard for surgical hair restoration. Unlike older strip-harvesting methods, FUE removes follicles one by one using a micro-punch tool, leaving only tiny circular marks that heal to near-invisibility within a few weeks.

The harvested follicles are sorted and prepared under magnification before being implanted into recipient sites — small incisions made at angles that mimic your natural hair growth direction. The result is a hairline and density that looks and behaves exactly like your own hair, because it is.

FUE is suitable for both men and women and can be used to restore the hairline, crown, temples, and beard. Because there is no linear scar, patients who prefer to wear their hair short are typically better candidates for FUE than for FUT (Follicular Unit Transplantation).

Key takeaway

FUE produces permanent, natural-looking results with no visible linear scar — making it the most chosen hair transplant method worldwide.

How FUE works Illustration

1

Consultation & design

Your surgeon maps the recipient area and designs the new hairline based on your facial proportions, age, and hair characteristics. A donor assessment confirms graft availability.

2

Preparation

Local anaesthetic is applied to both donor and recipient zones. Hair in the donor region (typically the back and sides of the scalp) is trimmed to 1–2mm.

3

Extraction

Individual follicular units (1–4 hairs each) are removed using a precision micro-punch tool (0.6–1.0mm). A typical session harvests 1,500–4,000 grafts over 6–8 hours.

4

Implantation

Recipient channels are created at precise angles and depths. Follicles are placed one by one to replicate natural density and growth direction.

5

Recovery begins

You leave the clinic with a post-operative care plan. Tiny scabs form over both areas; most patients return to desk work within 3–5 days.

Who is FUE right for?

FUE works best for patients who have a stable donor area and realistic expectations about what a transplant can achieve. Below is a plain-language guide — but a personalised assessment will always give you the clearest picture.

✓ Good candidates

  • Adults 25+ with stable hair loss (not actively progressing)
  • Norwood Scale I–VI with adequate donor density
  • Prefer to wear hair short (no linear scar)
  • Non-smokers or willing to stop 2 weeks pre-procedure
  • Realistic expectations — transplanted hair takes 9–12 months to fully grow
  • Good overall health with no autoimmune conditions

! May require extra assessment

  • Under 25 — hair loss pattern may not be fully established
  • Diffuse unpatterned alopecia (DUPA)
  • Alopecia areata — autoimmune cause needs separate treatment
  • Very fine or curly donor hair (not excluded, requires specialist)
  • Active scalp conditions (psoriasis, seborrhoea)
  • Taking blood thinners or certain medications

What to expect

Hair transplant results unfold over months, not days. Here is an honest, stage-by-stage guide to what your recovery and growth journey looks like.

Day
1–7

Immediately after

Small scabs, some swelling — this is normal

Tiny crusts form over each extraction and implantation site. Mild swelling around the forehead is common in the first 2–3 days. Most patients return to desk work within 3–5 days. Avoid touching the grafts.

2–4
wks

2–4 weeks

Shedding — expected, not a sign of failure

The transplanted hairs enter a resting phase and shed. This is called shock loss and is a completely normal part of the process. The follicles remain intact beneath the skin and will regrow.

1–3
mo

1–3 months

New growth begins

Fine, soft new hairs start to emerge from the transplanted follicles. Growth is uneven at this stage — some areas fill in faster than others. The donor area scars are barely visible.

6
mo

6 months

Significant coverage visible

Around 60–70% of the final density is typically visible by this point. Hairs are still maturing and thickening. Most patients begin to see the result they were hoping for around this milestone.

12
mo

12 months

Final result — permanent, natural growth

Full density and texture are reached. The transplanted hair behaves exactly like native hair — it grows, can be cut, styled, and coloured. Results are permanent because the follicles come from your DHT-resistant donor zone.

Side effects & risks

FUE is generally safe when performed by a qualified surgeon in a clinical setting. The risks below are real, evidence-based, and presented without minimisation.

Common (affects many patients)

Temporary swelling around forehead and eyes (days 2–5). Scabbing at donor and recipient sites. Shock shedding of transplanted hairs (weeks 2–4). Temporary numbness in donor area.

Uncommon (affects some patients)

Folliculitis (infected hair follicles) — manageable with antibiotics. Cyst formation. Temporary thinning of pre-existing hair near recipient zone. Uneven growth requiring touch-up procedure.

Rare (<2% of cases)

Keloid scarring (genetic predisposition). Persistent numbness. Significant graft failure. Allergic reaction to anaesthetic.

⚠ Provider quality matters

The majority of serious complications reported online are associated with under-regulated clinics, particularly in unregulated markets. Verify your provider's credentials through the Hairline platform before booking.

The procedure itself is performed under local anaesthetic and most patients report only mild discomfort during the initial injections. The extraction and implantation process is generally painless.
